Trade-promotion forecasting at retail scale

01 The problem

A major US retailer was losing millions annually to two opposite failure modes: stockouts during trade promotions (demand spikes the replenishment system didn't anticipate) and expiry losses when over-ordered perishable stock didn't sell through. Forecasting at the SKU × store level meant hundreds of thousands of individual demand series — far too many to model by hand, and too heterogeneous for one global model to serve well.

02 The finding

Per-series models, tuned and backtested automatically, beat both the incumbent system and a single global model — but only once the training loop itself became the product. The chart below shows forecast error (WAPE, lower is better) across approaches:

03 Decisions & tradeoffs

  • Per-series over global: heterogeneous demand patterns (promo-driven spikes vs. steady staples) made a single model a poor compromise; per-series won on accuracy at the cost of orchestration complexity — which automation absorbed.
  • PySpark on Databricks: the training loop hit a 0.94 parallelization factor, keeping 300k model fits inside a nightly window.
  • Automated backtesting as a gate: no model shipped without beating its own rolling-origin backtest — accuracy claims stay traceable.

    train, valid = rolling_origin_split(df)
    best = tune(train, param_grid, folds=3)

05 Measurement & limitations

The $2.4M figure comes from the client's own before/after accounting of stockout and expiry losses on covered categories — not from model metrics alone. Limitations worth being honest about: coverage was strongest on high-volume SKUs; long-tail series fell back to hierarchical pooling, and promo cannibalization effects were only partially modeled.
